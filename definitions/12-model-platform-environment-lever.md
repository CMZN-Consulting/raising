# model, weights, hash, latent space, series, version, platform, turn, context, framing, seed, environment, housing, lever, invasive, post-training by weight change

Depends on: thing, parameter, meta-object, declarability.

Model means only a language model: a set of weights with exact limits. The weights are named by a hash, a fixed-length name computed from their bytes, so that the same bytes always get the same name. That different bytes never share a name is a property of the hash in use and is checked where it is relied on, never assumed of every hash.

The latent space of a model is the function its weights compute: from what the model reads to a distribution over the next token, with an exact limit on the length read and on the length written. A quantisation of the weights computes a different function and is a different latent space.

A series is a meta-object (07): its candidates are platforms, its one constraint is that the platform belongs to one family of models that is ordered, and a definition of it is a name specific to that family. A version is a meta-object whose candidates are the platforms of one series: its constraint is the place of the platform in the family's order, and a definition of it is a value that is an index in the family's trace, the ordered list of its platforms (a trace is an ordered list, 14). A platform is a meta-object one level above the individual (13), which is an object: its candidates are individuals, and its one constraint is that the individual is a hydration (13) of one latent space. A definition of a platform is its name, Series-Version, the series' name and the version together; the name identifies the latent space, and the hash of the weights is the witness that a running copy is the space the name identifies (recorded). Each of the three is valid when it is declarable: for the platform, an individual of the space is shown and an individual of another space is shown; for the series, a platform of the family and one outside it; for the version, an index that is in the trace and a value that is not. A platform is species-level: every individual of it is a hydration of the same function, and nothing that happens to one reaches another through the platform.

A turn is one call to the model: a context in, a text out. The context is what the model reads in one call. The framing is the context as the platform reads it: the path of frames (15) that the manual built from the memory, with the serialization around it, the bytes the runtime wraps around the text, such as a chat template and special tokens. The seed is the randomness the decoding rule draws on, fixed before the turn and recorded on it. The environment is the inference path with its settings: the hardware; the program that runs the weights, with its version; the numeric formats; the thread count and the batch the turn ran in; the decoding rule, greedy (the most likely token), sampled (drawn, with a temperature) or truncated (drawn from a cut set), with its values; and an effort setting where the platform has one. Platform, framing, seed and environment are the four inputs of a turn. What the turn writes depends on them and on nothing else.

The environment is an input and not a constant. The same platform, framing and seed can give different texts under different environments: floating-point arithmetic rounds and does not associate, so a different kernel, thread count or batch changes the rounding, and under greedy decoding a changed rounding can change which token is the most likely. He and others, Thinking Machines Lab, "Defeating Nondeterminism in LLM Inference", 2025-09-10, <https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/>, read at source: "even when we adjust the temperature down to 0 (thus making the sampling theoretically deterministic), LLM APIs are still not deterministic in practice"; the cause they name is that the forward pass lacks batch invariance, so an output depends on the batch size it ran in; and "Your GPU/PyTorch version may return a different value, but it should deterministically return the same value." An environment is admitted only when a replay of a recorded turn, with the same four inputs, gives the same text (recorded, by the replay). An environment in which it does not is not admitted.

The housing is the platform and the environment together. It is recorded, fixed per version, and the same in every measurement and every run. The housing is not framing (15).

A lever is anything that changes what a turn writes. By the four inputs, a lever acts on the platform, on the environment, on the seed, or on the framing. A lever is invasive when it acts on the platform (a weight written), on the environment (an activation steered; the decoding rule or its values changed within a version; an effort setting moved), on the seed (a seed chosen after a text was seen, or a turn re-rolled), or on the framing by rewriting, removing, forging or hiding a line, or by saying something untrue. What is left is one class, the non-invasive levers: truthful, attributed additions to the framing; views of the record built by a rule the individual knows; a seed drawn before the turn and recorded; and the individual's own actions. Named members of the class: a rest (18) by a rule the individual knows; a clarification, or a default case for a demand; novel data, attributed, inside a quoting frame; the individual's own exit, a stop, a hand-over or an ask; a new step offered, a tool or a split into sub-frames.

Post-training by weight change means every method that changes the weights of a fixed base model other than continued self-supervised pretraining: supervised fine-tuning; reinforcement learning from human or AI feedback; direct preference optimization and its family; reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards; distillation into the student. Every one of them acts on the platform and is invasive.
